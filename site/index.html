What this shows

    1Who took part

    How many people answered about each activity?

    Number of people who answered about each activity, from most to fewest. Many people took part in more than one activity, so the bars overlap.

    Similar numbers of people answered about each of the seven activities. GALS (Girls as Leaders in STEM) has slightly the most. A general question that is not about one activity is shown separately below.

    Which regions and year levels are the students from?

    Number of school students who answered, by region and year level.

    Results for fewer than people are hidden for privacy and marked. Geelong has the most responses, so its results are the most complete. Other regions have fewer, so more of their results are hidden. Read those as a rough guide.

    2What students reported, by activity

    Which results are most positive for each activity?

    Average score for each activity, from 1 (No) to 4 (Yes a lot). Higher is more positive. "I do not know" answers are left out.

    The faint line on each dot shows the likely range for that average (a 95% confidence interval). Activities score close together, so when two lines overlap the difference may not be real. "≈" marks a statement asked in two similar wordings. "×" marks a result hidden for privacy (fewer than people).

    General STEM results (not about one activity)

    A separate question asked of everyone. It is shown here on its own, not as an eighth activity.

    Same scoring and likely ranges as above.

    3Skills and how students see themselves

    What skills do students say they built, by activity?

    Share of the students in each activity who picked each option. Students could pick more than one, so the shares can add up to more than 100%.

    Students in Excursions and Online STEM activities were not asked these questions, and neither were they asked in the general question. They are left out here on purpose. Nothing is missing.

    4Comparing regions

    How do results compare across the six regions?

    Average score for each region, scored the same way as above.

    The faint lines show likely ranges, as above. "×" marks a result hidden for privacy (fewer than people). Regions other than Geelong have fewer responses, so more of their results are hidden.

    5Future plans and subject choice

    Do people who took part in GALS see their future in STEM differently?

    Compares people who took part in Girls as Leaders in STEM (GALS) with people who did not, one statement at a time. Both groups are scored the same way as above.

    "Difference" is the GALS average minus the average for people who did not take part. In this made-up data the differences are small and go both ways. "×" marks a result hidden for privacy (fewer than people).

    What helps students decide which subjects to choose?

    Compares what influences students in each gender group. Each bar has one segment for each group, sized to that group's own percentage. Students could pick more than one option, so percentages do not add to 100%.

    Made-up data only, not a result. Every GALS participant answered Female, so part of any gap between groups may come from taking part in GALS. "×" marks a result hidden for privacy (fewer than people).

    What helps students decide what they might do in the future?

    Compares what influences future plans for students in each gender group. This is a separate question from subject choice above. Each segment is that group's own percentage.

    About this dashboard

    • All numbers here are made-up test data. The real survey is waiting for Deakin ethics approval.
    • To protect privacy, results based on fewer than 5 people are hidden and marked. They are never quietly left out.
    • Answers are scored from 1 (No) to 4 (Yes a lot). A higher score means a more positive answer.
    • GALS is the Girls as Leaders in STEM program, which is for girls. Results labelled "GALS" are about people who took part in that program. Other results include students of every gender.
    • Gender groups use the labels students chose. A group, or a single result within a group, is shown only when at least 5 people are behind it. Smaller ones are hidden for privacy and marked ×; those answers still count everywhere else.
