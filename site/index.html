2Outcomes by activity

Which outcomes do participants report most positively, by activity?

Average score per activity, on the survey's 1–4 scale. 4 = Yes a lot is the best answer; "I do not know" is excluded from every average.

The faint line through each dot is a 95% confidence interval: the activity types sit close together, and without it the differences between them look more certain than the sample size supports. Hover a dot for the exact interval and respondent count. "≈ wording variant" marks three outcome statements that appear twice with near-identical wording (a survey artifact: one activity's question block used slightly different phrasing than the rest). They are shown as separate rows for now, adjacent to their pair, so the pattern is visible rather than looking like missing data. "×" marks an activity/outcome combination with fewer than respondents in the current filter: suppressed, not plotted.

5Aspirations and subject choice

Does taking part in GALS shift how girls see their future in STEM?

Compares GALS participants to everyone else, item by item. Average score on the same 1–4 scale as the outcomes above (4 = Yes a lot is best); not tied to any one activity, so grouped by GALS participation rather than activity type.

The faint line through each dot is a 95% confidence interval, same reasoning as elsewhere on this page. The "Diff" column on the right is the GALS average minus the everyone-else average on the raw 1–4 scale; in this mock data it is small and mixed in direction (roughly ±0.2), which is expected, not a rendering issue: the generator has no built-in assumption that GALS improves aspirations, so a larger or more one-directional gap would be the surprising result. Read the difference alongside the confidence intervals, which usually overlap; that overlap is the honest signal that this sample cannot support a claim of significance either way. "×" marks a group/item combination with fewer than respondents: suppressed, not plotted.

What helps students decide which subjects to choose?

Compares which influences matter more to female students, and which matter more to male students. Each bar is one option, split into a female segment and a male segment placed side by side: segment length is that gender's own percentage, not a shared total, so read the number on each segment. Respondents could select more than one option, so percentages don't sum to 100% down the list.

The differences shown here are built into the mock data deliberately; they are not results. Female respondents include all GALS participants, since GALS is a girls' programme, so a gender gap across the whole sample partly reflects who took part in the programme rather than gender alone. That won't go away with real data either: any gender comparison here should be read alongside programme participation, not instead of it. A segment for a gender with fewer than respondents in the current filter shows "suppressed" instead of a length.

Do the STEM activities and programmes themselves shape these decisions?

How often "STEM activities and programmes" itself was picked as an influence on subject choice and on future plans, comparing GALS participants to everyone else. This option appears in both questions above; it gets its own panel here, since it's the closest thing in the survey to a direct measure of the programmes' own influence, rather than sitting as one row among ten.

The gap between the two groups here is built into the mock data deliberately, so the panel has something to show. It is not a result. When real data arrives this difference may be smaller, absent, or in the other direction. Same suppression rule as elsewhere: a group under respondents for a given question shows "suppressed" rather than a percentage.

Data notes

  • Source: respondents.csv, activity_ratings.csv, battery_selections.csv, aspirations.csv, subject_career.csv and open_text.csv from the reshaped Qualtrics export. If a future data drop removes any of these, the affected view shows an explicit empty state rather than stale or invented data.
  • "General STEM outcomes (not activity-specific)" is a real question block asked of everyone, shown on its own in the Outcomes section. It is not a real 8th activity, so it's excluded from every activity and region comparison.
  • respondents.csv ships a real did_gals column, used directly by the Aspirations section. An earlier data drop was missing it; the build falls back to deriving it from GALS activity participation if that happens again.
  • The scale runs 1 = No → 4 = Yes a lot, with "I do not know" tracked separately rather than as a fifth scale position. Higher is better and every average is used directly, with no inversion.
  • Cells under 5 respondents are suppressed and visibly marked throughout, never silently dropped or averaged.
  • Colours follow the real Deakin Dashboard Style Guide (Nov 2025); see site/css/tokens.css for sourcing notes.
  • The option "Subjects I need for a future job" is shown on this page as "Knowing which subjects I need for a future job": a display-only change from the raw survey wording, made at the team's request so it reads as being about knowing the requirement rather than the subjects themselves. The underlying data is unchanged.
